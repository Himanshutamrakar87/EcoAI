SDG 13 Climate Action Learning Center

Grounded in scientific consensus from the United Nations and the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

What is UN SDG 13?

Adopted by all 193 United Nations Member States in 2015, Sustainable Development Goal 13 calls for urgent action to combat climate change and its impacts. Targets include strengthening resilience to climate-related hazards, integrating climate measures into national policies, and improving human and institutional capacity on climate mitigation.

Global Warming vs. Climate Change

Global Warming is the long-term heating of Earth's climate system observed since the pre-industrial period due to human fossil fuel burning. Climate Change encompasses global warming plus the resulting broader shifts including melting glaciers, intensified precipitation extremes, shifting agricultural seasons, and rising sea levels.

Greenhouse Gases (GHGs)

Atmospheric gases that trap thermal infrared radiation: Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) from fossil fuels and deforestation; Methane (CH₄) from decomposing landfill organics and agriculture (28x warming potential of CO₂ over 100 yrs); and Nitrous Oxide (N₂O) from chemical fertilizers.

Impacts on Communities

Climate vulnerability disproportionately impacts coastal cities, agricultural food security, drinking water reservoirs, and public health through urban heat island effects, vector-borne diseases, and unpredictable monsoon patterns.

Campus & Student Climate Action

Colleges possess immense potential as living laboratories: conducting campus energy audits, implementing dual-stream waste segregation, switching to energy-efficient LED fixtures, establishing composting pits for canteen food waste, and promoting bicycle or mass-transit commutes.

1. Problem Statement

While climate change poses an existential threat, students and urban citizens often struggle to translate abstract scientific data into practical everyday habits. Information regarding carbon footprints, waste segregation, and energy conservation is frequently fragmented, overly complex, or difficult to contextualize to college environments.

2. Proposed Solution

EcoAI provides an intuitive, all-in-one web platform integrating Google Gemini AI reasoning with deterministic carbon formulas. The solution delivers interactive bilingual climate chat, personalized 30-day sustainability blueprints, instant waste sorting advice, verified carbon metrics, and gamified habit tracking that operates entirely within the browser.

3. Key Objectives

  • Democratize climate education aligned with United Nations SDG 13 targets.
  • Implement verified client-side numerical carbon auditing using IPCC and national grid emission baselines.
  • Provide accessible bilingual AI environmental guidance supporting both English and Hindi.
  • Design a responsive, zero-database architecture leveraging browser LocalStorage for privacy and fast local testing.

4. System Limitations

In strict compliance with the frontend-only project brief, API keys configured client-side are visible in browser developer tools (Network tab). Therefore, this build is tailored for academic project evaluation and local development. Commercial deployment would require an API gateway proxy. Additionally, calculations are educational approximations and do not constitute corporate ISO 14064 audits.

5. Future Scope

Future enhancements could include multi-campus leaderboard competitions, IoT-enabled smart electricity plug telemetry, computer-vision waste image identification using Gemini multimodal vision models, and localized municipal recycling pickup schedules.

Frontend-Only Security Architecture Disclosure

In compliance with BCA project requirements, EcoAI is built strictly as a static frontend with no backend server. Because requests to Google Gemini are made directly from the user's browser, any client-side API key can be inspected in browser Developer Tools. Never commit a real key to a public GitHub repository. For production web apps, requests must be proxied via a secure backend.
